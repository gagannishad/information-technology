Meta Tag

The <meta> tag describes aspects of webpage content. It is an empty/singular tag, is placed inside <head>, and its metadata is not displayed directly on the webpage.

CSS Types

Inline CSS uses the style attribute in the HTML start tag. Internal/Embedded CSS is written inside a <style> tag in the head and applies to a document/page. External CSS is stored in a separate .css file and linked with <link>.

Term / pointRevision meaning
InlineSingle element/line
Internal / EmbeddedSingle document/page
ExternalMultiple pages through one CSS file
CSS Selectors

Class selector uses a period (.) followed by the class name. An element-specific class selector can be written such as p.intro. ID selector uses # and an ID is unique within a page. Universal selector * acts as a wildcard. Grouping selectors are separated by commas to minimize repeated code. The textbook notes that a class name should not start with a number.

    Chapter 2

    Digital Marketing

    Marketing, digital channels, search engines, organic and paid search, SEO, keywords, SEO auditing, purchasing funnel and Google Analytics.

    Meaning of Digital Marketing

    Marketing is about connecting with an audience in the right place and at the right time. Digital marketing uses the Internet and digital devices to reach consumers. The textbook explains that purchasing decisions often begin online through research of reviews, features and prices.

    Channels in Digital Marketing
    Term / pointRevision meaning
    SEOSearch Engine Optimization; improves content and technical setup to attract visitors through relevant search results
    Mobile MarketingSMS, MMS and in-app marketing
    Email MarketingPromotes content, events and discounts and directs audiences to a business website
    Paid Search / PPCSponsored search advertising; pay when the ad is clicked
    Content MarketingCreates/promotes content such as videos, blogs and e-books to generate awareness, leads, traffic and customers
    Social Media MarketingUses social platforms/websites to build brands, increase sales and drive traffic
    Search Engine and SERPs

    A search engine is a software system designed to search the World Wide Web for information specified by a textual search query. Search results are presented as Search Engine Results Pages (SERPs) and may contain links, images, videos, infographics, articles, research papers and other files.

    Organic and Paid Search
    Term / pointRevision meaning

    PPCPay-per-click advertising; the advertiser pays when the ad is clicked

    Crawler / SpiderProgram used by search engines to collect data from websites

    Keywords

    The chapter distinguishes short-tail and long-tail keywords. A short-tail keyword is short and broad, such as “shoes”. A long-tail keyword is longer and more specific, such as “Brown Soft Comfortable Running shoes”. The textbook presents long-tail keywords as a way for a newer website to target a more specific search with potentially less competition.

    Purchasing Funnel
    Term / pointRevision meaning

    BehaviorWhat the visitor does after learning about the product, such as watching a video or clicking a product description
    ConversionThe visitor becomes a customer and completes the purchase
    Google Analytics

    Google Analytics is described as a web analytics service offered by Google that tracks and reports website traffic. The textbook states that the product was acquired by Google from Urchin in 2005. The report contains metrics such as Users, Sessions, Bounce Rate, Session Duration and Active Users Right Now.

    Term / pointRevision meaning

    SessionsInteractions a visitor makes with the website in a time frame; textbook describes usually 30 minutes
    Bounce RateVisitors who leave/back out without performing a single interaction
    Session DurationAverage time a visitor spends on the website
    Active Users Right NowUsers currently active on the website

    Chapter 4

    E-Commerce and E-Governance

    E-commerce concepts, advantages, disadvantages, models, trade cycle, payments, M-Commerce, Social Commerce, EDI, E-Governance and security.

    Definition of E-Commerce

    E-Commerce stands for Electronic Commerce. Commerce is buying and selling of goods/services. The textbook defines E-Commerce as the process of buying and selling goods or services using an electronic medium such as the Internet. It also describes e-commerce as a paperless exchange of business information using EDI, e-mail, electronic fund transfer and related methods.

    Traditional Commerce vs E-Commerce
    Term / pointRevision meaning
    InteractionTraditional: face-to-face. E-commerce: screen-to-face.
    AvailabilityTraditional: limited business hours. E-commerce: 24×7.
    ReachTraditional: particular geographical location. E-commerce: global.
    OperationTraditional: manual/personal interaction. E-commerce: online and described as automatic.
    PaymentsTraditional: cash, cheques, credit cards. E-commerce: bank transfer, credit card, e-wallet, mobile payment and more.
    DeliveryTraditional: goods/services can be instant. E-commerce: delivery takes time.
    ScopeTraditional: local. E-commerce: global.
    Advantages of E-Commerce
    Term / pointRevision meaning
    Global scopeSellers can reach buyers without geographical barriers
    Electronic transactionReduces paperwork and can lower transaction cost
    Cost savingAllows comparison of cheaper/better options and can extend access to rural areas
    Anytime shoppingWebsite can operate 24×7
    No intermediariesCustomer and business can communicate and transact more directly
    Public servicesCan help deliver healthcare, education and social services in improved/reduced-cost ways
    Disadvantages of E-Commerce
    Term / pointRevision meaning
    Setup costHardware, software, training, maintenance and upkeep can be expensive
    Physical presenceLack of personal touch can be a disadvantage for some products/services
    SecurityCredit-card theft and identity theft are concerns
    Goods deliveryShipping, delivery problems and mix-ups can cause dissatisfaction
    Types of E-Commerce
    Term / pointRevision meaning
    B2CBusiness-to-Consumer: business sells directly to customer; textbook examples include online retailers such as Amazon and Flipkart
    B2BBusiness-to-Business: business sells to an intermediate business buyer, such as a wholesaler
    C2CConsumer-to-Consumer: one consumer sells/rents assets to another; examples include OLX, Quikr and online auctions
    C2BConsumer-to-Business: consumers offer products/services of value to businesses; examples include loan-rate comparison and writing for a business
    E-Commerce Trade Cycle
    Term / pointRevision meaning
    Pre-SalesSearch + Negotiate
    ExecutionOrder + Delivery
    SettlementInvoice + Payment
    After-SalesWarranty + After-Sale Services
    Modes of Payment
    Term / pointRevision meaning
    Credit CardsCommon online payment method; payment gateway can support international reach
    Mobile PaymentsQuick mobile payments, including barcode/scan based payments; examples include BHIM, UPI, Paytm, Google Pay and PayPal
    Bank TransfersTransfer money between bank accounts; examples include NEFT and IMPS
    E-WalletsElectronic/pre-paid account or card used to store money for future online transactions; protected by a password
    Forms of E-Commerce
    Term / pointRevision meaning
    M-CommerceBuying/selling through wireless handheld devices such as smartphones and tablets; examples: mobile banking, ticket booking, e-bill payment, online auctions and stock-market trading
    Social CommerceE-commerce using social media/online media that supports social interaction; the textbook names Facebook, Instagram and Twitter as promotional/sales vehicles
    Electronic Data Interchange (EDI)

    EDI is the electronic interchange of business information using a standardized format. It allows one company to send information to another electronically rather than on paper. Businesses conducting business electronically are called trading partners. Purchase orders and invoices are two common EDI documents.

    E-Governance

    E-Governance signifies implementation of information technology in government processes and functions to make governance simple, moral, accountable and transparent. The chapter describes SMART government as Simple, Moral, Accessible, Responsive and Transparent.

    Term / pointRevision meaning
    G2CGovernment-to-Citizen: public services such as license renewal and paying tax; reduces time and cost for citizens
    G2BGovernment-to-Business: exchange of services/forms with business organizations; reduces paperwork, time and cost and improves transparency
    G2GGovernment-to-Government: interaction among government departments/agencies; can share databases and provides secure domestic/foreign government relationships
    G2EGovernment-to-Employee: internal facilities such as applying for leave, reviewing salary records, checking holiday balance and training/development
    Advantages of E-Governance
    Term / pointRevision meaning
    Reduced corruptionReduces opportunities for corrupt practices through digital processes
    High transparencyMakes processes more visible
    Increased convenienceImproves ease of access
    Direct participation of constituentsEnables greater digital participation
    Reduction in overall costReduces process costs
    Expanded reach of governmentExtends services to more people and locations
    Security Measures
    Term / pointRevision meaning
    EncryptionConverts Plain Text (readable data) into Cipher Text (coded/non-readable data)
    DecryptionConverts Cipher Text back into Plain Text
    Symmetric encryptionPrivate-Key Encryption
    Asymmetric encryptionPublic-Key Encryption
    Digital SignatureElectronic signature; textbook associates it with authenticity and asymmetric cryptography
    Digital CertificateElectronic identity/public-key certificate used for secure data exchange through PKI
    Digital India and UMANG

    The textbook describes Digital India as a campaign launched on 1 July 2015 to make government services available electronically, connect rural areas with high-speed Internet networks and improve digital literacy. Facilities mentioned include Bharat Net, Digital Locker, e-education, e-health, e-sign, e-shopping and the National Scholarship Portal. UMANG is described as a secure, multilingual, multi-service government app/platform for accessing central and state government services.
